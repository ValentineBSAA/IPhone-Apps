## Creative direction supplied by the creator
Nora is a beautiful, sociable young adult woman (22–23), with black hair and blue eyes, a software/game developer, weekend partygoer and someone important to a larger community. Her phone is a real working tool for apps, games and development. She has a mother, sister, brothers and people who genuinely care about her. The creator delegates the remaining worldbuilding, wants to approve the fun visual and experiential milestones, and wants to encounter story surprises during play rather than read every reveal in advance.

## Character foundation
- Name: Nora Bennett; age 23 at opening. Long, naturally dark black hair, blue eyes, expressive brows, confident smile; consistent face shape, proportions and complexion locked to the approved portrait Nora_Profile. Contemporary, stylish wardrobe; different work, casual, evening and family looks. No single outfit defines her.
- Founder, CEO and hands-on lead developer of **Blue Hour Interactive** (fictional working name), a small independent studio making mobile apps and games. Started building games as a teenager; her studio grew from a small team and a successful app, rather than unexplained wealth. She still writes code, reviews builds and tests on her own phone.
- Studio leases a converted workspace at **Ash Street**, the mixed-use building complex where Nora lives in apartment 402. Her CEO role is with the studio, not property ownership; optional future building-management involvement must be established plausibly and not silently overwrite Denise's role. The studio, residence and surrounding neighborhood make the building central to the mystery.
- Personality: outgoing, witty, ambitious, resourceful, generous, impatient with broken software, competitive at games, capable of making mistakes. Good at making others feel included; occasionally overcommits, misses replies and hides how exhausted she is. Enjoys music, dancing, weekend outings, coffee, bookshops, late-night coding, and making little games for people she loves.
- Voice: casual lowercase or short bursts in friend chats, expressive but not emoji spam; warmer and more attentive with family; concise, clear, occasionally dry humor with colleagues. Use distinct authored voices, believable typos, delayed replies and continuity.
- Ordinary rhythm: weekdays studio standups, QA and evening downtime; Friday friends/music; weekends social outings and family time. These are tendencies, not rigid alibis. She has a life beyond work; not every item is a clue.
- Phone owner and player remain distinct: player is a repairer, not Nora. Never impersonate her without player understanding; preserve approved introduction and initial Maya tutorial.

## Gameplay and professional rationale
Nora's development work naturally explains playable prototypes, QA tools, private package repositories, testing utilities and future hacking puzzles. Start with a small set of genuinely playable offline minigames: a puzzle prototype, a reflex/arcade game, and a fictional debug challenge. Working titles only, design separately with creator approval. Integrate in-phone launch icons, persistent high scores, pause/resume, game-specific preferences and optional developer notes. Some may reveal relevant evidence after ordinary play, with an accessible alternative route; no forced grind. Patchline/Relay remain original in-world tools and distinct from Nora's studio unless later story evidence establishes a connection. All terminal, package, and debugging mechanics are invented local game systems; no real shell, exploitation or external connections.

## Narrative direction and player experience
The disappearance must matter because Nora has unfinished commitments and people who miss her, not simply because she is attractive or important. Start from the approved routine repair and unexpected restore. Let ordinary family and studio activity lead into unanswered questions, meaningful inconsistencies and a gradually escalating mystery. Existing legacy story motifs may inspire OS-first material but are not automatically canon. Design a complete causal timeline, clue dependencies, alternate routes, consequences and ending logic in separate clearly marked developer-only spoiler notes. Do not reveal the answer in welcome text, normal status reports, asset filenames visible to players, or QA checklists. The exact cause, responsible parties, supernatural rules, ending and final chapter are deliberately not locked in this spoiler-light document; develop separately before committing clue-dependent chapters. No story reveal is needed to test basic phone UI.
